Guitar Basics For New Players

The first few weeks of learning guitar are mostly about your fingertips toughening up. It hurts to press steel strings against a fretboard until you build up calluses, and a lot of beginners quit right around that point thinking something is wrong with their technique when really it just takes time.

Learn open chords before you worry about anything fancy. G, C, D, E minor and A minor will let you play a huge number of songs once you can switch between them smoothly. Speed comes from practicing the transitions between chords, not from practicing each chord shape in isolation.

A metronome feels annoying at first but it fixes timing problems faster than almost anything else. Playing slightly out of time is the single biggest giveaway of a beginner guitarist, even more than sloppy chord changes, and a metronome trains your sense of rhythm without you having to think about it consciously.

Tune your guitar every single time you pick it up. Playing an out of tune guitar trains your ear to accept wrong pitches as normal, which makes it much harder to develop good musical instincts later on.
