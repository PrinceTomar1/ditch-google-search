What Are AI Agents

An agent, in the software sense, is a program that can look at its surroundings, decide on an action, and carry that action out on its own without a human clicking every button. The classic example is a thermostat: it senses the temperature, decides whether to turn the heat on, and acts. Modern AI agents do the same loop but with far richer inputs and far more complicated decisions.

A lot of the recent excitement around agents comes from pairing a language model with a set of tools it is allowed to call: searching the web, running code, reading a file, sending an email. The model reasons about what step to take next, calls a tool, reads the result, and decides whether it is done or needs another step. That loop of think, act, observe is basically the whole trick.

Building a reliable agent is harder than it sounds because small mistakes compound. If the model misreads a tool's output at step three, everything after that is built on a wrong assumption. Good agent systems spend a lot of effort on checking their own work, asking for confirmation before doing anything destructive, and giving the model a way to admit it is stuck.

Agents are not magic autonomous beings, they are closer to a very persistent intern who follows a loop, has access to a toolbox, and asks questions when the instructions are unclear.
