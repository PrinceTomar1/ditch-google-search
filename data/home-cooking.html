Getting Better At Home Cooking

Most home cooks improve faster by fixing their knife skills than by learning new recipes. A sharp knife and a comfortable grip make chopping onions or dicing carrots go from a chore to something almost relaxing, and uneven cuts are usually why vegetables cook unevenly in a pan.

Salt earlier than you think you need to. Salting pasta water, seasoning meat well before it hits the pan, and tasting as you go are the difference between a dish that tastes flat and one that tastes like something a restaurant would serve. Most beginner cooking mistakes are actually under-seasoning, not a bad recipe.

Learn a few base techniques instead of memorizing a huge list of recipes: how to properly sear a piece of meat, how to build a pan sauce from the browned bits left behind, how to cook rice without a rice cooker. Once you know the technique, you can improvise a meal out of whatever is in the fridge instead of needing a recipe card for every single dinner.

Cooking at home regularly is also just a matter of practice. The tenth time you make a stir fry it will come together in fifteen minutes without you thinking about it, even though the first attempt probably took an hour and dirtied every pan you own.
