A Short History of Space Exploration

Space exploration really kicked off in 1957 when the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, a satellite not much bigger than a beach ball. It didn't do much besides beep on a radio frequency, but it proved that getting an object into orbit was possible, and it set off a race that neither side was willing to lose.

The moon landing in 1969 is still the most famous moment in the whole story. Apollo 11 carried three astronauts to lunar orbit, and two of them walked on the surface while the world watched on grainy black and white television. The computers guiding that mission had less processing power than a modern calculator, which says a lot about how much of it came down to careful engineering and trained human judgment.

These days exploration has shifted toward robotic missions and reusable rockets. Rovers have been driving around Mars for years sending back soil samples and photos, and landing a booster rocket back on a floating pad has gone from a spectacular failure reel to a routine Tuesday.

The next big target most agencies talk about is a sustained human presence on the moon as a stepping stone toward eventually sending people to Mars, though the timelines for that keep sliding further into the future.
