Machine Learning Basics

Machine learning is a branch of computer science where a program improves at a task by looking at examples instead of being told exact rules. Rather than writing out every case by hand, you feed the algorithm a bunch of labeled data and let it figure out the patterns on its own.

Most beginners start with supervised learning, where every training example comes with the correct answer attached. The model makes a guess, compares it to the real answer, and nudges its internal numbers a little closer to being right. Do that over thousands of examples and the model slowly gets good at the task.

There is also unsupervised learning, which is used when you don't have labeled answers at all. Clustering algorithms, for instance, just look for groups of similar data points without anyone telling them what the groups should mean. It is messier than supervised learning but useful for exploring a dataset you don't understand yet.

Under the hood a lot of machine learning still comes down to linear algebra and gradient descent: nudging a big pile of numbers in the direction that reduces error. Neural networks are just a particular way of stacking those numbers into layers so the model can represent more complicated relationships between inputs and outputs.
