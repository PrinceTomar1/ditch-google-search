Gardening Tips For Beginners

The single biggest mistake new gardeners make is watering on a schedule instead of checking the soil. Stick a finger about two inches into the dirt before you water anything. If it still feels damp down there, skip that day. Overwatering kills more houseplants and vegetable beds than neglect ever does.

Sunlight matters more than most people expect. Tomatoes and peppers want at least six hours of direct sun, while leafy greens like lettuce and spinach are happy with a few hours of shade in the hottest part of the afternoon. Walk your yard at different times of day before you decide where the garden bed goes.

Soil is not just dirt, it is food. Working compost into a bed before you plant anything gives roots something to actually feed on, and it improves drainage in heavy clay soil at the same time. A layer of mulch on top holds moisture in and keeps weeds from taking over your vegetable patch.

Start small. A raised bed of four square feet with a couple of tomato plants, some herbs, and a row of beans will teach you more about gardening than an ambitious quarter acre that turns into a weekly chore you dread.
