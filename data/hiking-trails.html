Picking Your First Few Hiking Trails

New hikers almost always overestimate how far they can comfortably go. A flat three mile trail feels very different from three miles with two thousand feet of elevation gain, so check the elevation profile of a trail before you check the distance, since elevation is what actually determines how hard a hike feels.

Water is the thing people forget most often. A rough rule of thumb is about half a liter per hour of moderate hiking in mild weather, more if it is hot or the trail is steep. Running out of water two miles from the trailhead turns a pleasant afternoon into a genuinely bad day fast.

Tell someone your route and your expected return time before you head out, especially on a trail you haven't hiked before. Cell service disappears in a lot of canyons and forests, and a simple text to a friend with your trailhead and planned finish time is the cheapest safety measure that exists.

Start with trails that have good reviews for being well marked. Getting lost is rarely dangerous on a busy, well signed trail, but it can turn serious fast on a faint path in the backcountry, so save the ambitious routes for once you have more experience reading a map and a compass.
